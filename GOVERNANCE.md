# CRLN-TRG-001 — Governance

**Status:** v0.1, 9 September 2026. This document is published to make §6 of the standard
checkable rather than asserted.

§6 of CRLN-TRG-001 states that "a Methodology Review Board, conflict-of-interest policy, and
appeals process govern updates." Until today those three things existed as a sentence and not
as documents. This file is the correction: it describes what actually exists, what does not
exist yet, and who currently decides — including where that is a single person.

An adopter should be able to read this and know exactly how much independent oversight sits
behind the framework today. The answer is: less than the sentence implied.

---

## 1. Who decides today

**Custodian:** Clinical Research Learning Network (CRLN), a sole-founder organisation.
**Named decision-maker:** Joshua Webber, founder.

Every version of this framework published to date was authored and approved by one person.
There is no standing board. Saying otherwise in §6 was inaccurate and that sentence is
scheduled for correction in the next release (§7 below).

Two independent checks do exist and are load-bearing:

- **Subject-matter expert review.** Framework changes affecting competency content are
  reviewed by external clinical research SMEs, with a 2-of-3 consensus requirement and
  agreement against the authored key. This gate is real and has rejected content.
- **Change control.** Published versions are immutable, each carries its own DOI, and every
  change is recorded in `CRLN-TRG-001-CHANGELOG.md` before release.

**Still required to constitute.** A chair, elected by the members rather than appointed by the
custodian, and three further members. At least one must bring psychometric or
assessment-methodology expertise, and at least one must come from a low- or middle-income
country research setting. Until the Board reaches quorum, changes continue to be approved by
the custodian on SME review and every release says so in the change log.

**Purpose.** To approve or reject proposed changes to the competency set, the rating scale,
the domain weights, and the regulatory crosswalk.

**Composition, when constituted.** Five members: at least three practising clinical research
professionals covering site, sponsor and regulatory perspectives; at least one member with
psychometric or assessment-methodology expertise; at least one member from a low- or
middle-income country research setting. A majority must be unaffiliated with CRLN.

**Quorum and decision rule.** Three members, simple majority, with dissents recorded in the
change log. The custodian holds no casting vote on competency content.

**Term.** Two years, renewable once.

**Until it exists.** Changes are approved by the custodian on SME review. Every release states
plainly, in the change log, that it was approved this way.

## 3. Conflict of interest

**Status: policy in force for the custodian; register open, one member entry recorded.**

**Register.**

1. **Jeffrey Smyth** (seated 2026-09-15), declared on the day of acceptance:
   *President and Executive Director of TrueBlue Clinical Research, an independent
   clinical research site.* TrueBlue is an adopter of CRLN's measurement platform.
   Under rule 2 below, he does not vote on any change whose effect falls
   differently on independent sites than on networks or sponsors. The disclosure
   was volunteered without being asked for, which is recorded here because the
   register is only worth anything if it shows what members declare unprompted.

**Standing disclosures.**

- CRLN publishes the framework openly under CC BY 4.0 and also operates a commercial
  measurement platform that implements it. **The custodian therefore has a direct commercial
  interest in the framework's adoption.** This is the central conflict and it cannot be
  removed while both activities sit in one organisation; it can only be disclosed and
  constrained.
- The framework and crosswalk are openly licensed. The measurement engine, item parameters
  and learner data are proprietary. A change to the framework that advantaged CRLN's
  implementation over another adopter's would be a governance failure, and is the specific
  risk the Board exists to check.
- CRLN has received no industry sponsorship, and no sponsor, CRO or vendor has funded,
  reviewed or influenced any published version to date.

**Rules.**

1. Members and reviewers disclose financial or professional interests in any organisation
   that would be materially affected by a proposed change, before deliberating on it.
2. A member with a disclosed interest in a specific change does not vote on that change.
3. Disclosures are published alongside the release they applied to.
4. SME reviewers are compensated for time only, at a flat rate, never contingent on the
   outcome of their review.

## 4. Proposing a change

Anyone may propose one. No account, affiliation or fee is required.

1. **Raise it** — open an issue at the framework's public repository, or email
   `standard@crln-learn.com`. State the competency or section affected, what is wrong, and
   the regulatory or evidential basis for the change.
2. **Acknowledgement** — within 10 working days, with a decision on whether it proceeds to
   review.
3. **Review** — SME review where the change affects competency content; custodian review for
   editorial, citation or structural changes.
4. **Decision** — accepted, accepted with modification, deferred, or declined, with reasons
   published in the change log.
5. **Release** — accepted changes appear in the next version, with the proposer credited
   unless they ask otherwise.

Corrections of fact — a mis-cited regulation, a renumbered section — are treated as urgent and
do not wait for a scheduled release. Version 1.1.3 was exactly this: 38 of 48 domains cited
ICH E6(R2) section numbers while labelled R3.

## 5. Appeals

**Status: process defined, never yet invoked.**

If a proposal is declined and the proposer believes the decision was wrong:

1. Request review within 60 days, stating the grounds.
2. Once the Board exists, the appeal is heard by members who took no part in the original
   decision. Until then, the custodian obtains a fresh SME opinion from a reviewer not
   involved in the original decision and publishes both that opinion and the outcome.
3. The outcome is recorded in the change log whether or not the appeal succeeds.

An appeal has never been filed. When one is, this section will say so.

## 6. Versioning

Semantic, and applied to meaning rather than to word count:

| change | example | version |
|---|---|---|
| **Major** — competencies added or removed, scale changed, weights changed | a new domain | 2.0 |
| **Minor** — a competency's wording materially changes what is assessed | narrowing a behaviour | 1.2 |
| **Patch** — citations corrected, editorial fixes, companion artifacts | the R2 → R3 renumbering | 1.1.3 |

Published versions are immutable. A published version is never edited; a correction is a new
version. Each receives its own DOI.

**Citation.** Cite the **concept DOI** — [10.5281/zenodo.22049549](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22049549)
— to refer to the framework in general; it always resolves to the latest version. Cite a
version DOI to pin to a specific release. Note that
[10.5281/zenodo.22049550](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22049550) is **version 1.1**, not the
concept DOI; the change log labelled it as the concept DOI until 9 September 2026, and anyone
who followed that instruction has pinned a citation to v1.1 believing they cited the framework
generally.

## 7. Known gaps

Listed because a governance document that only describes strengths is marketing.

- **No independent board.** One person approves changes. §2 defines the Board; it is not
  constituted.
- **§6 of the standard overstates this.** The published text asserts a Board, a COI policy and
  an appeals process as though all three were operating. Two of the three were aspirational.
  The next release corrects that sentence to point here.
- **The custodian's commercial interest is structural**, not incidental, and is disclosed
  rather than resolved.
- **The regulatory crosswalk is partly provisional.** Of 200 jurisdictions mapped, 39 are
  confirmed against a named authority and 161 fall back to the ICH baseline. Provisional
  entries are flagged as such wherever they are surfaced.
- **No adoption registry.** There is currently no published list of who has adopted the
  framework, so adoption cannot be independently verified.
