# Translations

**English is authoritative.** Where a translation and `../STANDARD.md` differ,
the English governs.

These are **unofficial**. They were produced with machine assistance and have not
yet been reviewed by a native-speaking clinical research professional. That is
stated plainly on every file rather than buried, because a mistranslated
competency statement in a framework somebody mandates is a real problem and not a
cosmetic one. ICH publishes its own translations on the same basis.

They cover the **adoptable core**: purpose and scope, the ten canonical domains,
the role tracks, and the licence and maturity notices. They deliberately do not
translate the standards crosswalk tables, because ICH E6(R3) section numbering and
JTF identifiers are language-independent and translating the surrounding prose
without the authority to reinterpret a regulatory citation would add risk without
adding use.

## Why translations exist at all

Clinical research training is overwhelmingly published in English. So is almost
every competency framework. Both are barriers that fall hardest on the workforce
that trials increasingly depend on, in exactly the countries hosting a growing
share of them.

A ministry, university or provider can adopt a framework it can read. It cannot
adopt one it cannot.

## Corrections

A correction from someone who works in clinical research in that language is the
fastest way to improve these, and the most welcome contribution this repository
can receive. Open a pull request or an issue. Credit is given in `CHANGELOG.md`
unless you ask otherwise.
