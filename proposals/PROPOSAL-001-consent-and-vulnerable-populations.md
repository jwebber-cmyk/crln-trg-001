# Proposal 001: consent competencies for vulnerable populations and non-standard consent

**Status: PROPOSAL. Not adopted. Not in any released version of CRLN-TRG-001.**

This document exists to be argued with. It proposes adding competencies to the framework,
which is a MINOR version change under the framework's own versioning rules. The Methodology
Review Board has one seated member and has not reached quorum, so the custodian cannot and
will not adopt this alone.

Raised 2026-09-22 · against CRLN-TRG-001 v1.7.2 · `standard@crln-learn.com`

---

## The evidence: three independent crosswalks converged

Three crosswalks were built in sequence against unrelated source frameworks. None was looking
for consent. All three landed on it.

| Crosswalk | What it found |
|---|---|
| **TDR Global Competency Framework** (WHO/UNICEF/UNDP/World Bank, 2016) | *Engaging with the community* absent entirely. TDR added it specifically for low-resource and community-based research. |
| **ICH E6(R3) Annex 2** (in force 2027-01-15) | §3.4.2 *Consent and permission considerations for RWD* absent. Every CRLN consent statement is trial-participant consent. |
| **Regulation (EU) No 536/2014** | Articles 30, 31, 32, 33 and 35 absent: cluster trials, incapacitated subjects, minors, pregnant and breastfeeding women, emergency situations. |

Three sources, three traditions, one gap. That is stronger evidence than any single reviewer
could give, and it is why this proposal exists rather than a smaller correction.

## The gap, stated plainly

CRLN-TRG-001 has **four** informed-consent competencies across four role tracks:

- `CRLN-CRC-D3` Informed Consent Process
- `CRLN-PI-D2` Informed Consent Oversight
- `CRLN-RN-D2` Obtaining & Documenting Informed Consent
- `CRLN-DCT-D2` Remote & Electronic Informed Consent

**Every one assumes a competent adult consenting for themselves.**

Consent by a legal representative, a minor's assent, deferred consent in an emergency, and
simplified consent in a cluster trial are absent from all four, and from every other track.

This is the wrong way round. Consenting a healthy volunteer who understands the protocol is
the *easy* case. The competencies that distinguish a capable coordinator from an unsafe one
are exactly the ones missing.

## What is proposed

Draft statements only. Wording, placement and weighting are all open.

**C1 · Consent by a legal representative.** The individual can identify when a subject lacks
capacity, establish who may lawfully consent on their behalf in the applicable jurisdiction,
document that authority, and recognise when capacity has returned and consent must be sought
again from the subject. *(EU CTR Art 31)*

**C2 · Assent and consent for minors.** The individual can obtain and document parental or
guardian consent, seek the minor's assent proportionate to age and understanding, recognise
and act on a minor's refusal, and manage re-consent when a minor reaches the age of majority
during a trial. *(EU CTR Art 32)*

**C3 · Deferred and emergency consent.** The individual can recognise a situation meeting the
criteria for deferred consent, act within the protocol's emergency provisions, and obtain
consent from the subject or their representative as soon as practicable thereafter, with
correct documentation of why consent was deferred. *(EU CTR Art 35)*

**C4 · Cluster and simplified consent.** The individual understands when simplified consent
applies in a cluster-randomised trial and what it does and does not permit. *(EU CTR Art 30)*

**C5 · Community engagement.** The individual can engage a community affected by a trial
before and during it, recognise where community-level permission is expected alongside
individual consent, and identify when a consent process designed elsewhere is not
appropriate locally. *(TDR competency 39)*

**C6 · Consent and permission for secondary use of existing data.** The individual can
distinguish consent to participate from permission to use pre-existing health data, and
recognise when the latter is required. *(ICH E6(R3) Annex 2 §3.4.2)*

## The structural problem this creates, which is a reviewer's decision

**Every role track currently has exactly six domains, D1 to D6. 14 tracks × 6 = 84.** That
symmetry is not decorative: domain codes are positional within a track, and the scoring rules
and weights assume six.

There is no painless way to add these. The options, none of which the custodian should pick
alone:

1. **Extend affected tracks to D7 and beyond.** Breaks the symmetry and any consumer that
   assumed six. Weights must be renormalised.
2. **Replace an existing domain in the affected tracks.** Keeps six, but removes something
   currently assessed, and every historical score against the replaced domain becomes
   incomparable.
3. **Add a separate conditional module** applied only to trials involving these populations,
   scored outside the six. Preserves comparability, but creates a second scoring path.
4. **Fold them into the existing consent domain** as additional anchors rather than new
   domains. Cheapest, and arguably dishonest, because it hides a real competency difference
   inside one score.

The custodian's view, offered as a view and not a decision: option 3 is most likely correct,
because these competencies genuinely do not apply to every trial, and pretending they do
would penalise a coordinator who has never run a paediatric study.

## Questions for reviewers

1. Is the gap real, or is it correctly out of scope for a framework aimed at trial execution?
2. Which of the four structural options, and why?
3. Does C5 belong in this proposal at all? It is a community competency, not a consent one,
   and it may deserve its own.
4. Which role tracks should carry each statement? RN is the obvious home for C2, and is
   currently silent on paediatrics.
5. Is anything here dangerous to assess? A coordinator scored 3 of 4 on emergency consent is
   still a coordinator making that decision at 3am.

## What happens next

Nothing, until reviewers respond. If adopted this is a MINOR release, not a patch, and it
requires the Methodology Review Board to have reached quorum or an explicit, recorded
custodian decision on SME review with the reviewers named.
