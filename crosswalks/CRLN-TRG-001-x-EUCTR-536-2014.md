# CRLN-TRG-001 × Regulation (EU) No 536/2014: a crosswalk

**Status: PROVISIONAL and UNREVIEWED.** One person's judgement. No EU regulatory
professional, no subject-matter expert and no member of the Methodology Review Board has
checked it. Corrections: `standard@crln-learn.com`.

CRLN-TRG-001 version 1.7.1 · generated 2026-09-22 · id `CRLN-TRG-001-X-EUCTR-536-2014`

## Why this one mattered most

A citation audit of all 84 CRLN competency statements found roughly **37 references to US
federal sources** (21 CFR, FDA guidance, Form 1572) and **one** to Regulation (EU) No
536/2014, the law binding in every EU Member State since 31 January 2022.

A framework describing itself as the global standard while citing one jurisdiction forty
times and another once is not global. This closes that gap on the largest single omission.

Source: the Commission's published text. <https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/reg_2014_536_en_0.pdf>

## Article 49 is the article this framework exists to serve

**Article 49, "Suitability of individuals involved in conducting the clinical trial",**
requires that individuals conducting a trial be qualified by education, training and
experience. Like ICH E6, it does not define qualification in any form a person can measure.

That undefined requirement is the reason CRLN-TRG-001 exists. **Until this document, the
framework never cited it.**

## Scope of this crosswalk

The Regulation has 99 articles. Most govern Member State procedure, the
EU portal and database, comitology, and inter-State cooperation, which impose no duty on an
individual in a trial role. **32 articles that do impose such duties** were
assessed. The rest are out of scope for a competency framework, not overlooked.

## The finding: consent is only mapped for the person who can give it

**Five of the nine gaps are consecutive articles, and they are all the same problem.**

| Article | |
|---|---|
| 30 | Informed consent in cluster trials |
| 31 | Clinical trials on incapacitated subjects |
| 32 | Clinical trials on minors |
| 33 | Clinical trials on pregnant or breastfeeding women |
| 35 | Clinical trials in emergency situations |

CRLN-TRG-001 has four separate informed-consent competencies across four role tracks. Every
one of them assumes a competent adult consenting for themselves.

**Consent by a legal representative, a minor's assent, deferred consent in an emergency, and
simplified consent in a cluster trial are absent from the framework entirely** — including
from the Research Nurse track, where paediatric assent would most obviously live.

These are the situations where consent competence actually matters. A coordinator who can
consent a healthy volunteer is not thereby competent to consent an unconscious patient's
representative, and the framework currently cannot tell those apart.

The remaining four gaps are narrower: auxiliary medicinal products (Art 46) and the
Qualified Person (Art 62) are EU statutory concepts with no CRLN equivalent, the
Investigator's Brochure (Art 55) has no competency despite being cited elsewhere, and
damage compensation (Art 76) is absent exactly as it was in the TDR crosswalk.

## One partial worth reading as a gap

**Article 52, reporting of serious breaches.** CRLN assesses protocol deviations well. But
"serious breach" is a defined concept in the Regulation carrying a **seven-day notification
clock**, and no CRLN competency teaches or assesses that distinction or that deadline.

## Notes

**Art 28 · General rules on protection of subjects** · full  
Subject-protection duties map across CRC, PI and RA.

**Art 29 · Informed consent** · full  
Strong across four tracks.

**Art 30 · Informed consent in cluster trials** · none  
NO COUNTERPART. Cluster-randomised trials and their simplified consent route are absent from CRLN.

**Art 31 · Clinical trials on incapacitated subjects** · none  
NO COUNTERPART. Consent by legal representative for incapacitated adults is absent.

**Art 32 · Clinical trials on minors** · none  
NO COUNTERPART. Assent, paediatric consent and the minor's wishes are absent from every track, including RN.

**Art 33 · Clinical trials on pregnant or breastfeeding women** · none  
NO COUNTERPART. No competency addresses this population.

**Art 35 · Clinical trials in emergency situations** · none  
NO COUNTERPART. Deferred and emergency consent is absent, and it is the hardest consent situation a coordinator meets.

**Art 36 · Notification of start of trial and end of recruitment** · partial  
CRLN covers start-up operationally but not the CTR's specific notification duties and clocks.

**Art 37 · End, temporary halt and early termination** · partial  
CRLN has no close-out or early-termination competency as such.

**Art 38 · Temporary halt or early termination for subject safety** · partial  
Escalation is covered; the sponsor's halt decision and its notification clock are not.

**Art 41 · Reporting of adverse events and SAEs by the investigator** · full  
Strong across CRC, PI, RN and ASSIST.

**Art 42 · Reporting of SUSARs by the sponsor** · full  
Strong via the PV track.

**Art 43 · Annual reporting by the sponsor to the Agency** · partial  
CRLN covers periodic reporting generically, not the CTR's annual safety report to EMA.

**Art 46 · Reporting with regard to auxiliary medicinal products** · none  
NO COUNTERPART. 'Auxiliary medicinal product' is an EU CTR concept with no CRLN equivalent.

**Art 47 · Compliance with the protocol and good clinical practice** · full  
Strong. This is the CTR's GCP-compliance article.

**Art 48 · Monitoring** · full  
Strong via the CRA track.

**Art 49 · Suitability of individuals involved in conducting the clinical trial** · full  
THE ARTICLE THIS FRAMEWORK EXISTS TO SERVE. Art 49 requires that individuals be qualified by education, training and experience, and like ICH E6 does not define it measurably. That undefined requirement is CRLN-TRG-001's entire reason for existing, and until now the framework never cited it.

**Art 50 · Suitability of clinical trial sites** · full  
Maps to the CRA feasibility competency.

**Art 51 · Traceability, storage, return and destruction of IMPs** · full  
Strong across PHARM, CRC and CRA.

**Art 52 · Reporting of serious breaches** · partial  
CRLN covers deviations, but 'serious breach' is a defined EU CTR concept with a seven-day notification clock that CRLN does not teach or assess.

**Art 53 · Other reporting obligations relevant for subject safety** · partial  
Generic escalation only.

**Art 54 · Urgent safety measures** · partial  
CRLN covers escalation but not the specific urgent-safety-measure duty and its notification.

**Art 55 · Investigator's brochure** · none  
NO COUNTERPART. No competency addresses maintaining or using the IB, though ICH E6 Appendix A is cited elsewhere.

**Art 56 · Recording, processing, handling and storage of information** · full  
Strong, though CRLN frames it via 21 CFR Part 11 rather than the CTR.

**Art 57 · Clinical trial master file** · full  
Strong via the CTA track.

**Art 58 · Archiving of the clinical trial master file** · partial  
CRLN covers TMF management; the CTR's 25-year archiving duty is not assessed.

**Art 62 · Responsibilities of the qualified person** · none  
NO COUNTERPART. The Qualified Person is an EU statutory role with no CRLN track. This is a structural gap for EU operations.

**Art 66 · Unauthorised IMPs and auxiliary medicinal products: labelling** · partial  
CRLN cites EU GMP Annex 13 once; CTR Annex VI labelling is not assessed.

**Art 71 · Sponsor** · partial  
CRLN assesses individuals, not the sponsor entity, so this maps only where a person discharges it.

**Art 73 · Principal investigator** · full  
Strong via the PI track.

**Art 76 · Damage compensation** · none  
NO COUNTERPART. Insurance and indemnity are absent from CRLN, as they were from the TDR crosswalk.

**Art 93 · Data protection** · partial  
One statement on one track. The CTR points at GDPR, which CRLN cites once across 84 statements.

## What this implies for the framework

Recorded, not actioned. The Methodology Review Board is below quorum and cannot approve a
change of this size.

1. Vulnerable-population and non-standard consent competencies are missing and would be a
   MINOR version change at least. They belong across CRC, PI and RN rather than in one place.
2. Article 49 should be a normative reference in the specification, alongside ICH E6(R3).
3. Serious breach, with its seven-day clock, is a distinct competency from protocol deviation.
